Maktab Programme · Placement & Progress

Standardized Recitation Assessment

A gated placement instrument for the Maktab recitation class — administered on paper by a Qari and two helpers in one session, producing a per-student profile rather than a single number.

Draft v0.1 Status not yet given to any student Total 20 + 34 + 24 points Session ~100 min · 25 students · 3 adults
Before printing. Every āyah reference below is given from memory and marked precisely so the check is quick — none has been verified against a muṣḥaf. The ḥadīth wordings carry their narrator and collection but should be confirmed from a printed source. This is text that will be handed to children.

Three jobs, in order of importance. Place each student in a teaching track on day one, so nobody is taught ikhfāʾ before they can tell خ from ح. Profile each student, so the Qari knows what to teach this group. Baseline each student, so the same instrument given again measures growth rather than opinion.

PART 0

Screening

20 points · gate

0A · Letter recognition — audio to text

The teacher plays seven recordings in the order below. Each student's sheet lists the same seven letters in a different order; the student writes the clip number beside the letter they heard. Every letter is recorded with a fatḥah — a bare consonant cannot be uttered.

Play order · 7 items
PlayClipLetterNear neighbour it must not be confused with
1screen-a-1شَس
2screen-a-2مَن
3screen-a-3خَح
4screen-a-4تَط
5screen-a-5ءَع
6screen-a-6ذَز ظ
7screen-a-7قَك

These seven span the whole mouth — ء deep throat, خ lower throat, ق back of tongue, ش middle of tongue, ت tongue tip, ذ tip against the front teeth, م lips. Each has a near neighbour a weak student confuses it with, so a correct answer is unlikely to be luck.

0B · Word recognition — audio to text

Play order · 7 items
PlayClipWordWhat it tests
1screen-b-1مُحَمَّدٌshaddah + tanwīn
2screen-b-2تُحِيطُواْtwo madd letters + a silent alif
3screen-b-3ٱلسَّمَآءِhamzat waṣl + sun lām + madd
4screen-b-4أَعۡمَٰلٌsukūn + dagger alif + tanwīn
5screen-b-5ٱقۡرَأۡhamzat waṣl + two sukūns
6screen-b-6مُؤۡمِنِينَhamzah on a seat + madd yāʾ
7screen-b-7كَتَبَthree plain open syllables — the easy one

0C · Read aloud

Matching alone does not prove reading. A student can match by word shape and length without decoding a single letter. So the screen ends with three words read out loud. This runs as the first thirty seconds of the practical station, so it costs no extra queue.
#PromptLooking for
1بِسۡمِletters + kasrah + sukūn, no guessing
2قُلۡ هُوَsukūn on the lām, clean هُوَ
3ٱلَّذِينَhamzat waṣl, shaddah, madd yāʾ
0cannot decode
1decodes with effort, or one error
2fluent and correct

The gate

Pass requires 0A ≥ 5 and 0B ≥ 5 and 0C ≥ 4. A student who fails is placed at Level 0 — Foundations and stops here: he is not asked to sit a written paper he cannot read. Record the sub-scores anyway — they are the baseline he will be measured against.

PART 1

Theory

34 points · ~25 min

Written, whole group. Items marked CORE form a short form of 16 for younger students or a compressed session.

D1 · The virtue of learning the Qurʾān 5 pts

T1.1CORE The Prophet ﷺ said that the one proficient with the Qurʾān — ٱلۡمَاهِرُ بِٱلۡقُرۡآنِ — is with:
  • a) the prophets
  • b) the noble, righteous scribes — ٱلسَّفَرَةِ ٱلۡكِرَامِ ٱلۡبَرَرَةِ
  • c) the martyrs
  • d) his family
T1.2CORE The one who reads haltingly — يَتَتَعۡتَعُ فِيهِ — and finds it hard has:
  • a) no reward until he improves
  • b) one reward
  • c) two rewards
  • d) exactly the same as the proficient one
T1.3 True or false: because the struggling reader gets two rewards, he is better than the proficient reader.
  • FALSE — the proficient one's rank, with the angels, is the higher one. The two rewards are for the reading and for the hardship.

This item exists because the opposite is a very common misunderstanding.

T1.4CORE ٱقۡرَأۡ وَٱرۡتَقِ وَرَتِّلۡ كَمَا كُنۡتَ تُرَتِّلُ فِي ٱلدُّنۡيَا — his rank (مَنۡزِلَة) is at:
  • a) the first āyah he reads
  • b) the last āyah he reads
  • c) the longest sūrah he knows
  • d) how many times he finished the Qurʾān
T1.5 The ḥadīth مَا ٱجۡتَمَعَ قَوۡمٌ فِي بَيۡتٍ مِنۡ بُيُوتِ ٱللَّهِ يَتۡلُونَ كِتَابَ ٱللَّهِ وَيَتَدَارَسُونَهُ بَيۡنَهُمۡ names four things that happen to them. Write any three.
  • tranquillity descends · mercy covers them · the angels surround them · Allah mentions them to those with Him

Max 1 point for this question.

Sources. T1.1–1.3 Bukhārī & Muslim, from ʿĀʾishah. T1.4 Abū Dāwūd & Tirmidhī, from ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr. T1.5 Muslim, from Abū Hurayrah. Confirm the exact Arabic from a printed source before this goes to print.

T6.1CORE The letters of ٱلِٱسۡتِعۡلَاء are collected in خُصَّ ضَغۡطٍ قِظۡ — how many? → 7 — خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ
T6.2CORE Which of these is always heavy?

  • b) ص

RESULT

Scoring and placement

The level comes from the practical; the theory refines it.

LevelConditionTeaching track
0 — Foundationsscreening failsQāʿidah: letters, ḥarakāt, sukūn, joining
1 — Readerscreening passes, practical ≤ 8fluency, madd ṭabīʿī, ghunnah by ear
2 — Applyingpractical 9–17nūn sākinah and madd, named and applied
3 — Consolidatingpractical 18–24, theory < 24close the theory gaps; waqf; longer passages
4 — Advancedpractical ≥ 21 and theory ≥ 27itqān, rules of ر and لام, peer tutoring
Why the practical leads. A child taught by ear can recite ikhfāʾ perfectly and not know the word. That is a vocabulary gap, and it closes in one lesson. The reverse — naming every rule and reciting them wrong — is the real problem, and placing on theory would hide it.

The level is the least useful output. The profile is what the Qari should get: seven theory sub-scores and three practical sub-scores per student, with the class averages beside them. If eighteen of twenty-five students lose the same point on iqlāb, that is next week's lesson.

SESSION

Running it

~100 min

Staff: the Qari at the one-to-one station, plus two helpers for proctoring and marking. Materials: one speaker loud enough for the room, fourteen audio clips, and three printed sheets per student. A stopwatch is not needed — ḥarakāt are judged by ear.

Recording the fourteen clips. Use the app's own intake tool at #/intake — it already records unprocessed 16-bit WAVs and derives the filenames. Record all fourteen in one sitting, in your own voice, so the room and the level are constant across the set.
25 students · 3 adults
MinWhat
0–10Settle, explain, hand out screening sheets
10–25Screening 0A + 0B — whole group, each clip played twice
25–35Helpers mark 0A + 0B against the key while the Qari starts the queue
35–60Theory paper — whole group. Level-0 students go to a side activity with a helper
25–100One-to-one station, continuous — screening 0C plus the practical, ~5 min each
100+Collect, tally, file

The one-to-one station runs underneath everything else from minute 25 — that is the only way twenty-five students fit into one session. A second grading adult halves it.

Three things that will go wrong

The room is too loud to hear the clips
Play each clip twice. If the back row still cannot hear, the screening result is about the room, not the child — mark it and re-run rather than record a false Level 0.
A student cannot read the English on the theory paper
Note it. That is a language result, not a tajwīd result, and conflating the two is the fastest way to make this instrument wrong.
A parent asks what the level means
Give them the profile, not the number.

NOTES

Decisions, and what I could not answer

Settled with the author

Makhraj of the madd letters — confirmed
The five general makhārij are ٱلۡجَوۡف · ٱلۡحَلۡق · ٱللِّسَان · ٱلشَّفَتَان · ٱلۡخَيۡشُوم. There is no صدر among them: the madd letters come from ٱلۡجَوۡف. T2.5 keys ٱلۡجَوۡف and keeps "the chest" as a distractor.
Madd lengths — confirmed
4 ḥarakāt for muttaṣil and munfaṣil, 6 for lāzim — matching the app's own timing table.
Theory does not gate the practical — confirmed
Only the screening gates. Oral proficiency routinely arrives before the vocabulary for it: a child drilled by a Qari for two years may recite ikhfāʾ correctly and never have heard the word. Gating on theory would send that child to Level 1 and waste him.
The third practical ruling — confirmed
Idghām with ghunnah and idghām without ghunnah — ل ر. Items P9 and P10.
Recording minors — decided
No student audio is captured. They are minors; recording brings parental consent, retention and storage into a session that needs none of it. The Qari grades live.

Still open

Age range
The English reading level of the theory paper assumes about ten and up. If the class includes seven-year-olds, read the core-16 short form aloud rather than handing it over.
Class size and staff
The timetable assumes 25 students and 3 adults. Give me the real numbers and I will re-cut it.

Keeping results comparable across years

Two rules, free now and impossible to retrofit. Stamp the version on every result — when an item is reworded next year the version changes, and anyone comparing across versions is warned instead of misled. Never renumber an item — T3.6 is that item forever; a dropped item's number is retired, not reused. Item numbers are keys, not positions.

What has not been verified

The āyah references are from memory and unchecked — they are given precisely so the muṣḥaf check takes ten minutes rather than an hour. The ḥadīth wordings carry their narrator and collection but the exact Arabic should be confirmed from print. And no item has been trialled: after the first administration you will find items every student gets right, which measure nothing, and items that turn out ambiguous. That is what a pilot is for.
