# Maktab Recitation Assessment — draft v0.1

A standardized placement and progress assessment for the Maktab recitation
programme. Written to be given on paper by a Qari and two helpers in a single
session, and to produce a *profile* — not just a number.

**Status: draft for the author's review.** Nothing here has been given to a
student yet. Section 9 lists the decisions I made that you may want to reverse,
and the questions I could not answer for you.

---

## 1. What this is for

Three jobs, in order of importance:

1. **Place** each student in a teaching track on day one, so the Qari is not
   teaching ikhfa to a child who cannot yet tell خ from ح.
2. **Profile** each student — per-topic sub-scores, so the Qari knows what to
   teach *this* group rather than what to teach in general.
3. **Baseline** each student, so the same instrument given again in a few
   months measures growth rather than opinion.

Job 3 is the one that fails silently if the instrument changes between
administrations. See §8.

## 2. Shape

```
  Part 0  SCREENING          group (A, B) + 1-to-1 (C)      20 pts
             │
             ├── fail ──►  Level 0 — Foundations.  STOP.  No theory, no practical.
             │
             ▼ pass
  Part 1  THEORY             group, paper, proctored        34 pts
  Part 2  PRACTICAL          1-to-1 with the Qari           24 pts
             │
             ▼
          PROFILE + LEVEL
```

The screening is the only hard gate. **Theory does not gate the practical** —
see §9.1 for why, since this differs from the brief.

## 3. Part 0 — Screening

### 3A. Letter recognition — audio to text (7 items, group)

The teacher plays seven recordings in the fixed order below. Each student has
a sheet listing the same seven letters in a *different* order, and writes the
clip number next to the letter they heard.

Each letter is recorded **with a fatḥah** — a bare consonant cannot be uttered.

| Play order | Clip | Letter |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `screen-a-1` | شَ |
| 2 | `screen-a-2` | مَ |
| 3 | `screen-a-3` | خَ |
| 4 | `screen-a-4` | تَ |
| 5 | `screen-a-5` | ءَ |
| 6 | `screen-a-6` | ذَ |
| 7 | `screen-a-7` | قَ |

**Answer key** (sheet order): ء → 5 · ت → 4 · ذ → 6 · خ → 3 · ش → 1 · ق → 7 · م → 2

*Why these seven.* They span the whole mouth — ء deep throat, خ lower throat,
ق back of tongue, ش middle of tongue, ت tongue tip, ذ tip against the front
teeth, م lips. Each also has a near neighbour a weak student confuses it with
(خ/ح, ق/ك, ش/س, ت/ط, ذ/ز/ظ, م/ن), so a correct answer is unlikely to be luck.

### 3B. Word recognition — audio to text (7 items, group)

Same method, seven words.

| Play order | Clip | Word | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `screen-b-1` | مُحَمَّدٌ | shaddah + tanwīn |
| 2 | `screen-b-2` | تُحِيطُواْ | two madd letters + a silent alif |
| 3 | `screen-b-3` | ٱلسَّمَآءِ | hamzat waṣl + sun lām + madd |
| 4 | `screen-b-4` | أَعۡمَٰلٌ | sukūn + dagger alif + tanwīn |
| 5 | `screen-b-5` | ٱقۡرَأۡ | hamzat waṣl + two sukūns |
| 6 | `screen-b-6` | مُؤۡمِنِينَ | hamzah on a seat + madd yāʾ |
| 7 | `screen-b-7` | كَتَبَ | three plain open syllables (the easy one) |

**Answer key** (sheet order): ٱقۡرَأۡ → 5 · أَعۡمَٰلٌ → 4 · ٱلسَّمَآءِ → 3 · تُحِيطُواْ → 2 · كَتَبَ → 7 · مُؤۡمِنِينَ → 6 · مُحَمَّدٌ → 1

### 3C. Read aloud (3 items, 1-to-1, ~30 seconds)

**Matching alone does not prove reading.** A student can match by word shape and
length without decoding a single letter. So the screen ends with three words
read *out loud*, scored 0–2 each. This runs as the first thirty seconds of the
practical station, so it costs no extra queue.

| # | Prompt | Looking for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | بِسۡمِ | letters + kasrah + sukūn, no guessing |
| 2 | قُلۡ هُوَ | sukūn on lām, clean هُوَ |
| 3 | ٱلَّذِينَ | hamzat waṣl, shaddah, madd yāʾ |

**0** = cannot decode · **1** = decodes with effort or one error · **2** = fluent and correct

### Screening gate

| | |
|---|---|
| Maximum | 7 + 7 + 6 = **20** |
| **Pass** | A ≥ 5 **and** B ≥ 5 **and** C ≥ 4 |
| Fail | → **Level 0 — Foundations.** Stop here. Do not administer Part 1 or Part 2. |

A student who fails is not asked to sit a written paper he cannot read. Record
the screening sub-scores anyway — they are the baseline he will be measured
against.

## 4. Part 1 — Theory (34 points)

Written, whole group, ~25 minutes. Items marked **[C]** are the **core 16** —
a short form for younger students or a short session.

### D1 · The virtue of learning the Qurʾān (5 pts)

**T1.1 [C]** The Prophet ﷺ said that the one *proficient* with the Qurʾān
(ٱلۡمَاهِرُ بِٱلۡقُرۡآنِ) is with —
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) the prophets &nbsp; b) **the noble, righteous scribes — ٱلسَّفَرَةِ ٱلۡكِرَامِ ٱلۡبَرَرَةِ** ✔ &nbsp; c) the martyrs &nbsp; d) his family

**T1.2 [C]** The one who reads haltingly (يَتَتَعۡتَعُ فِيهِ) and finds it hard has —
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) no reward until he improves &nbsp; b) one reward &nbsp; c) **two rewards** ✔ &nbsp; d) exactly the same as the proficient one

**T1.3** *True or false:* because the struggling reader gets two rewards, he is
**better** than the proficient reader.
&nbsp;&nbsp;**FALSE** ✔ — the proficient one's rank (with the angels) is the higher one.
The two rewards are for the reading and for the hardship.
*(This item exists because the opposite is a very common misunderstanding.)*

**T1.4 [C]** «ٱقۡرَأۡ وَٱرۡتَقِ وَرَتِّلۡ كَمَا كُنۡتَ تُرَتِّلُ فِي ٱلدُّنۡيَا» — his rank (مَنۡزِلَة) is at —
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) the first āyah he reads &nbsp; b) **the last āyah he reads** ✔ &nbsp; c) the longest sūrah he knows &nbsp; d) how many times he finished the Qurʾān

**T1.5** The ḥadīth «مَا ٱجۡتَمَعَ قَوۡمٌ فِي بَيۡتٍ مِنۡ بُيُوتِ ٱللَّهِ يَتۡلُونَ كِتَابَ ٱللَّهِ وَيَتَدَارَسُونَهُ بَيۡنَهُمۡ»
names four things that happen to them. Write any **three**.
&nbsp;&nbsp;✔ tranquillity descends (نَزَلَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلسَّكِينَةُ) · mercy covers them (غَشِيَتۡهُمُ ٱلرَّحۡمَةُ) ·
the angels surround them (حَفَّتۡهُمُ ٱلۡمَلَائِكَةُ) · Allah mentions them to those with Him (ذَكَرَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ فِيمَنۡ عِنۡدَهُ)
&nbsp;&nbsp;*1 point per correct item, max 1 for this question.*

> Sources: T1.1–1.3 Bukhārī & Muslim (from ʿĀʾishah). T1.4 Abū Dāwūd & Tirmidhī
> (from ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr). T1.5 Muslim (from Abū Hurayrah).
> **Verify the exact Arabic wording against a printed source before printing.**

### D2 · Makhārij (6 pts)

**T2.1 [C]** How many *general* places of articulation (ٱلۡمَخَارِجُ ٱلۡعَامَّة) are there?
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) 3 &nbsp; b) **5** ✔ &nbsp; c) 7 &nbsp; d) 17
&nbsp;&nbsp;*(ٱلۡجَوۡف · ٱلۡحَلۡق · ٱللِّسَان · ٱلشَّفَتَان · ٱلۡخَيۡشُوم — containing 17 specific makhārij.)*

**T2.2 [C]** Match each part of the throat to its two letters:
| | |
|---|---|
| أَقۡصَى ٱلۡحَلۡق — deepest | **ء هـ** ✔ |
| وَسَطُ ٱلۡحَلۡق — middle | **ع ح** ✔ |
| أَدۡنَى ٱلۡحَلۡق — nearest the mouth | **غ خ** ✔ |

**T2.3 [C]** Which group is entirely from **the lips**?
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) **ف ب م و** ✔ &nbsp; b) ب م ن &nbsp; c) و ي ا &nbsp; d) ف ث ذ

**T2.4** The letter from the **deepest part of the tongue** (أَقۡصَى ٱللِّسَان) is —
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) ك &nbsp; b) **ق** ✔ &nbsp; c) ج &nbsp; d) ي

**T2.5 [C]** The madd letters ا و ي come out of —
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) the throat &nbsp; b) the tongue &nbsp; c) **ٱلۡجَوۡف — the empty space of the mouth and throat** ✔ &nbsp; d) the chest

**T2.6** The ghunnah comes out of —
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) **ٱلۡخَيۡشُوم — the nasal passage** ✔ &nbsp; b) the throat &nbsp; c) the lips &nbsp; d) the tongue

### D3 · Nūn sākinah and tanwīn (9 pts)

**T3.1 [C]** How many rules are there for نۡ and tanwīn? → **4** ✔

**T3.2 [C]** The letters of **إِظۡهَار** are — → **ء هـ ع ح غ خ** ✔ *(the throat letters)*

**T3.3 [C]** The word **يَرۡمَلُونَ** collects the letters of — → **إِدۡغَام** ✔
&nbsp;&nbsp;Which **two** of them take **no** ghunnah? → **ل ر** ✔ *(the other four, يَنۡمُو, take ghunnah)*

**T3.4** **إِقۡلَاب** has how many letters, and which? → **one — ب** ✔
&nbsp;&nbsp;The nūn becomes a hidden **م** with ghunnah.

**T3.5** How many letters of **إِخۡفَاء**? → **15** ✔ *(everything not in the other three rules)*

**T3.6 [C]** Name the rule in each — *(1 pt each, 5 pts)*
| | Ref | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| مِنۡ خَيۡرٍ | 2:105 | **إظهار** ✔ |
| فَمَن يَعۡمَلۡ | 99:7 | **إدغام بغنة** ✔ |
| مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡ | 2:5 | **إدغام بغير غنة** ✔ |
| سَمِيعٌۢ بَصِيرٌ | 22:61 | **إقلاب** ✔ |
| أَنفُسَكُمۡ | 2:44 | **إخفاء** ✔ |

### D4 · Madd (5 pts)

**T4.1 [C]** The natural madd (ٱلۡمَدُّ ٱلطَّبِيعِيّ) is held for ___ ḥarakāt → **2** ✔

**T4.2 [C]** A madd letter followed by a hamzah **in the same word** is
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ **مَدّ مُتَّصِل** ✔, held **4** ḥarakāt

**T4.3 [C]** A madd letter at the **end of a word**, hamzah at the **start of the next**
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ **مَدّ مُنۡفَصِل** ✔, held **4** ḥarakāt

**T4.4 [C]** A madd letter followed by a **sukūn or shaddah**
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ **مَدّ لَازِم** ✔, held **6** ḥarakāt

**T4.5** Classify each — *(1 pt for all five correct)*
| | Ref | Type |
|---|---|---|
| جَآءَ | 110:1 | مُتَّصِل |
| إِنَّآ أَعۡطَيۡنَٰكَ | 108:1 | مُنۡفَصِل |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | لَازِم |
| سُوٓءَ | 16:59 | مُتَّصِل |
| قَالُوٓاْ إِنَّا | 2:14 | مُنۡفَصِل |

### D5 · Ghunnah (3 pts)

**T5.1 [C]** Every **نّ** and **مّ** (carrying a shaddah) must be given ghunnah,
held ___ ḥarakāt → **2** ✔

**T5.2 [C]** Which of these carries a **complete ghunnah** (غُنَّة كَامِلَة)?
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) مِنۡ خَيۡرٍ &nbsp; b) **إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ** ✔ &nbsp; c) قُلۡ &nbsp; d) بِسۡمِ

**T5.3** Ghunnah is a quality of which two letters, and where does it live?
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ **ن and م**, in **ٱلۡخَيۡشُوم** ✔

### D6 · Letters of tafkhīm (4 pts)

**T6.1 [C]** The letters of ٱلِٱسۡتِعۡلَاء are collected in **خُصَّ ضَغۡطٍ قِظۡ** —
how many are there? → **7** ✔ *(خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ)*

**T6.2 [C]** Which of these is **always** heavy?
&nbsp;&nbsp;a) س &nbsp; b) **ص** ✔ &nbsp; c) د &nbsp; d) ت

**T6.3** Pair each light letter with its heavy partner:
&nbsp;&nbsp;س → **ص** · ت → **ط** · ذ → **ظ** · د → **ض** ✔

**T6.4** ر is **heavy** when it carries ___ and **light** when it carries ___
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ heavy with **fatḥah or ḍammah**; light with **kasrah** ✔
&nbsp;&nbsp;*(The full rules of ر are a lesson of their own. This item tests the basic case only.)*

### D7 · The signs in the muṣḥaf (7 pts)

**T7.1 [C]** Match each waqf sign to its meaning — *(1 pt each, 3 pts for any 3 correct)*
| Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **مـ** | you **must** stop — ٱلۡوَقۡفُ ٱللَّازِم |
| **لا** | **do not** stop — ٱلۡوَقۡفُ ٱلۡمَمۡنُوع |
| **ج** | stopping is **permitted** — جَائِز |
| **صلى** | **continuing** is better — ٱلۡوَصۡلُ أَوۡلَىٰ |
| **قلى** | **stopping** is better — ٱلۡوَقۡفُ أَوۡلَىٰ |
| **∴ ∴** | ٱلۡمُعَانَقَة — stop at **one** of the two, never both |

**T7.2 [C]** The wavy sign **ٓ** above a letter tells you to → **lengthen it** ✔

**T7.3 [C]** The **round** zero — ٱلصِّفۡرُ ٱلۡمُسۡتَدِير (e.g. the alif of قَالُواْ) means the letter is
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ **never pronounced — whether you stop or continue** ✔

**T7.4 [C]** The **rectangular** zero — ٱلصِّفۡرُ ٱلۡمُسۡتَطِيل (e.g. أَنَا۠) means the letter is
&nbsp;&nbsp;→ **pronounced if you stop on it, dropped if you continue** ✔

**T7.5** In مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَٰسِطٍ, reading straight through — do you pronounce the final
alif of أَنَا۠? → **No** ✔ &nbsp; And if you stop on أَنَا۠? → **Yes** ✔

**T7.6** *(stretch)* **س / سكتة** tells you to → **cut the sound briefly without
taking a breath, then continue** ✔ *(Ḥafṣ has four.)*

**Theory total: 34** · **Core-16 short form: 16**

## 5. Part 2 — Practical (24 points)

One-to-one with the Qari. ~5 minutes per student including screening 3C.
The student is shown the phrase in print and asked to recite it.

**Scoring, every item, 0–2:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **2** | correct and consistent |
| **1** | present but wrong length, inconsistent, or visibly effortful |
| **0** | absent or wrong |
| **NR** | not reached — the block was stopped early. **NR is not 0.** |

**Error tags** — circle rather than write:
`SHORT` `LONG` `NO-GHUNNAH` `EXTRA-GHUNNAH` `MAKHRAJ` `HESITANT`

**Adaptive stop:** if a student scores **0 on the first two items of a block**,
skip the rest of that block and mark the remainder `NR`. This saves the queue
and spares the student.

### P1 · Ghunnah (3 items, 6 pts)

| # | Prompt | Ref | Listening for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسۡرٍ | 103:2 | nūn mushaddadah held ~2 ḥarakāt, nasal |
| 2 | ثُمَّ كَلَّا سَوۡفَ تَعۡلَمُونَ | 102:4 | mīm mushaddadah, and the lām mushaddadah **not** given ghunnah |
| 3 | عَمَّ يَتَسَآءَلُونَ | 78:1 | mīm mushaddadah + (bonus) the muttaṣil in يَتَسَآءَلُونَ |

### P2 · Madd (4 items, 8 pts)

| # | Prompt | Ref | Listening for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | إِذَا جَآءَ نَصۡرُ ٱللَّهِ | 110:1 | **muttaṣil**, 4 |
| 5 | إِنَّآ أَعۡطَيۡنَٰكَ ٱلۡكَوۡثَرَ | 108:1 | **munfaṣil**, 4 |
| 6 | وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | **lāzim**, 6 — clearly longer than item 4 |

*Item 7 is the single best discriminator in the whole practical. A student who
lengthens everything equally scores 2 on items 4–6 by accident; item 7 catches it.*

## 6. Scoring and placement

Enter three numbers and five sub-scores. The level comes from the practical;
the theory refines it.

| Level | Condition | Teaching track |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Foundations** | screening fails | Qāʿidah: letters, ḥarakāt, sukūn, joining |
| **1 — Reader** | screening passes, practical ≤ 8 | fluency, madd ṭabīʿī, ghunnah by ear |
| **2 — Applying** | practical 9–17 | nūn sākinah + madd, named and applied |
| **3 — Consolidating** | practical 18–24, theory < 24 | close the theory gaps; waqf; longer passages |
| **4 — Advanced** | practical ≥ 21 **and** theory ≥ 27 | itqān, rules of ر and لام, peer tutoring |

**Why the practical leads.** A child taught by ear can recite ikhfāʾ perfectly
and not know the word. That is a vocabulary gap, and it closes in one lesson.
The reverse — naming every rule and reciting them wrong — is the real problem,
and placing on theory would hide it.

**The level is the least useful output.** The profile is what the Qari should
get: seven theory sub-scores and three practical sub-scores per student, and
the class averages beside them. If 18 of 25 students lose the same point on
iqlāb, that is next week's lesson.

## 7. Running the session

### Staff and materials

- **3 adults**: the Qari (1-to-1 station), plus 2 helpers (proctoring, marking).
- One speaker loud enough for the room; **14 audio clips** for 3A and 3B.
- Printed: one screening sheet, one theory paper, one practical grid per student.
- A stopwatch is *not* needed. Ḥarakāt are judged by ear.

> **Recording the 14 clips.** Use the app's own intake tool — open `#/intake`,
> which already records unprocessed 16-bit WAVs and derives the filenames. It
> was built for this exact job. Record them in your own voice, in one sitting,
> so the room and the level are constant across all fourteen.

### Timetable — 25 students, 3 adults, ~100 minutes

| | Minutes | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–10 | Settle, explain, hand out screening sheets |
| 2 | 10–25 | **Screening A + B** — whole group, clips played twice each |
| 3 | 25–35 | Helpers mark A+B against the key while the Qari starts the 1-to-1 queue |
| 4 | 35–60 | **Theory paper** — whole group. Level-0 students go to a side activity with a helper |
| 5 | 25–100 | **1-to-1 station**, continuous — screening C + practical, ~5 min each |
| 6 | 100+ | Collect, tally, file |

The 1-to-1 station runs *underneath* everything else from minute 25 — that is
the only way 25 students fit in one session. Two stations (if a second
qualified adult can grade) halves it.

### Things that will go wrong, and the answer

- **The room is too loud to hear the clips.** Play each clip twice; if the back
  row still cannot hear, the screening result is about the room, not the child.
  Mark it and re-run rather than record a false Level 0.
- **A student cannot read the English on the theory paper.** Note it. That is a
  *language* result, not a tajwīd result, and conflating the two is the
  fastest way to make this instrument wrong.
- **A parent asks what the level means.** Give them the profile, not the number.

## 8. Keeping the results comparable

Two rules, both of which are cheap now and impossible to retrofit:

1. **Stamp the version on every result.** A result records
   `assessmentVersion: "0.1"`. When an item is reworded next year, the version
   changes, and anyone comparing across versions is warned instead of misled.
2. **Never renumber an item.** `T3.6` is that item forever. If it is dropped,
   its number is retired, not reused. Item numbers are keys, not positions.

*(Both of these are the same rule this project already learned about word ids
and calibrations — a key that shifts silently corrupts every record that points
at it.)*
